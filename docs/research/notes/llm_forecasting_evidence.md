# LLM Forecasting Evidence: Benchmarks, Papers, Techniques, and Whether LLMs Can Beat Market Prices (as of 2026-11-16)

Scope note: Brier score = mean squared error of probability vs 0/1 outcome (lower is better; always-50% = 0.25). "Peer score"/"baseline score" on Metaculus are log-score-based (higher is better). "Brier Index" on ForecastBench is a rescaled, difficulty-adjusted Brier where higher is better, and its anchor moves over time, so values from different dates can't be compared. Everything below is dated. Live trading arenas and hobbyist bots are covered by another researcher and only mentioned in passing here.

## Overall question: Can an LLM forecasting system plausibly beat prediction-market prices (the bar for trading profit)?

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, no peer-reviewed or well-controlled study shows an LLM system reliably beating **live, liquid** market prices net of costs. The best systems now **match** human superforecasters on ForecastBench and Metaculus Pros on FutureEval (statistical ties in mid-2026). Against prices, the record is: roughly at parity on aggregate Brier, worse near resolution and on consensus markets, sometimes better early in a market's life and on toss-up markets, and useful as an **ensemble input alongside** the market price. Simulated betting returns are mostly below break-even. The only positive return figures are small and hypothetical.

### Cited Findings
**Head-to-head vs market prices (Brier or skill score)**
- **Prophet Arena (data to Oct 11, 2025; 1,367 events / 72,136 Kalshi-sourced binary markets, 76% sports):** GPT-5 (reasoning) Brier 0.184 (±0.006) vs market baseline 0.187. Grok-4 0.189, Claude Sonnet 4 0.194, Gemini 2.5 Flash 0.197, Llama 4 Scout 0.219. Calibration (ECE): GPT-5 0.042, Claude Sonnet 4 0.041 vs market 0.069. All five selected LLMs were better calibrated than the market. — [Prophet Arena paper, arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)
- **Prophet Arena simulated betting:** average return per $1 (break-even = 1.0): GPT-5 0.943 (±0.042), Claude Sonnet 4 0.909, market baseline 0.899, Gemini 2.5 Flash 0.883, Grok-4 0.864, Llama 4 Scout 0.805. No model broke even, so good calibration did not become profit. — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)
- **TimeSeek (Apr 2026; 150 Kalshi markets resolving Oct 2025–Jan 2026; 10 frontier models; 5 checkpoints per market; 15,000 predictions):** Brier Skill Score (BSS) is measured against the contemporaneous market price, so positive means beating the market. Pooled over checkpoints with search, **every model was negative**: Claude Opus 4.5 was best at −0.068 and Qwen3-235B worst at −0.615. At Open+1, four models beat the market: Claude Opus 4.5 +0.167, Kimi-k2.5 +0.118, GPT-5.2 +0.085, Kimi-k2 +0.011. At Close-1, all models were far below it (−0.73 to −1.74). Model–market error correlations were r = 0.19–0.41. The best pair, Claude + Kimi-k2.5, improved pooled BSS to −0.041 but still did not beat the market. No trading returns were modelled. — [TimeSeek, arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220)
- **AIA Forecaster (Bridgewater AIA Labs, Nov 2025):** on "MarketLiquid" (1,610 questions from a liquid public prediction market), market consensus Brier was 0.1106 vs AIA Forecaster 0.1258 vs OpenAI o3 0.1324. The market won. A leave-one-out ensemble weighted ~0.67 market / 0.33 AIA reached **0.106**, beating both, so the LLM added information. In a live prospective test on closed markets (n = 64, Aug 2025), AIA scored 0.1002 vs market 0.1111. The authors say n = 64 is too small to distinguish the two. — [AIA Forecaster tech report, arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1); [11-analyses synthesis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **AIA Forecaster on ForecastBench market questions (FB-Market):** AIA 0.0753 vs superforecaster median 0.0740 vs "market consensus" 0.0965 vs public median 0.1035. Here the market number is the price snapshot ForecastBench supplies, not a live price. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)
- **"The Copier Line" (EA Forum, data as of Aug 11, 2026):** ForecastBench market questions show forecasters a price that is about 10 days old. That shown price scored Brier 0.0843, and the price on the due date scored **0.0767** (3,175 questions). Of 527 leaderboard entries, **442 score significantly worse than the shown market price** (Benjamini–Hochberg, 5% FDR), and **none beat it after correction**. Uncorrected, only Cassi (p = 0.004) and the superforecaster median (p = 0.017) beat it. 204 of 273 leaderboard entries (75%) score at or below the "copier line". Baseline models scored a Brier Index of 51.5 without the market price and 68.1 with it, still below the 70.93 shown-price line. 77 of 531 forecast sets correlate above 0.95 with the shown prices. — [The Copier Line, EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- GPT-4.5's ForecastBench forecasts correlated **0.994** with the market prices it was shown (FRI figure, cited by Good Judgment). The FT found an AI forecaster "relied heavily on market-based trackers" for Fed decisions and drew level with the market without beating it. — [Good Judgment, "Not So Fast" (Jul 24, 2026)](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/not-so-fast)

**Simulated / real trading returns**
- **Turtel et al. (Lightning Rod Labs), "Outcome-based RL to Predict the Future" (TMLR, Nov 2025):** RL with a negative-Brier reward on ~10k resolved Polymarket questions plus ~100k synthetic questions. Each forecast was turned into a hypothetical one-share bet at the Polymarket price at the prompt timestamp. v3 reports **$52 profit vs $39 for o1, ~10% ROI, ~20% ROI on questions where the market was less confident**. v2 used a different trading rule and reported $127 vs $92. **Polymarket remained more accurate than the models**. On questions where the market was confident, the best model was not statistically better than chance at betting against it. Test-set size differs between versions (3,132 vs 1,265 questions). — [arXiv 2505.17989v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17989v3)
- **"When do prophets profit in prediction markets?" (Gu, Kagan, Sun, Wu, Xu; UChicago / Kalshi Research / NYU Shanghai; v3 Sep 2026; NeurIPS 2026 poster):** For order-book markets and any proper scoring rule S, the authors define a "proper betting" strategy that depends only on the forecast p and price q. It earns **positive expected profit whenever p beats q under S, given sufficient liquidity**, and they call it essentially the only strategy with that robustness guarantee. Across thousands of AI forecasts it was the only strategy that reliably turned accuracy into profit. A month-long live Kalshi pilot was "encouraging", but the abstract gives no figures. — [alphaXiv 2607.06166](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.06166)
- **"Beyond Forecasting: The Belief-to-Trade Layer" (Wang et al., Jul 3, 2026):** argues that forecasting benchmarks leave out the trading layer (selection, sizing, risk control) or hardcode it. It introduces Raven-Agent and a replay protocol that tests the trading layer separately from the forecaster, and reports better return on stake and risk-adjusted return in replay. Only the abstract and introduction were verified. — [arXiv 2607.03015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03015)

### Inferences
- The fair bar for trading is the **live, liquid price at trade time**. Many headline "beats the market" numbers use a stale snapshot instead: FB-Market prices are about 10 days old, and the fresher due-date price scores 0.0767 vs 0.0843. On liquid markets with contemporaneous prices (AIA's MarketLiquid, TimeSeek pooled, Prophet Arena returns), LLMs lose or roughly tie.
- The most defensible way to make money is **not** "LLM replaces market". It is: (a) combine the LLM forecast with the market price (AIA ensemble 0.106 vs market 0.111), (b) trade selectively where LLMs show an edge (early in a market's life, toss-up prices, long horizons), and (c) size bets with a proper-betting/Kelly-style rule. Each element has some support, but no study tests the full pipeline out of sample with real fees and slippage.
- Calibration is not edge. LLMs can be better calibrated than markets (Prophet Arena ECE 0.042 vs 0.069) and still lose money. Profit needs **discrimination** that the price does not already contain.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study with real-money LLM trading returns of meaningful size, net of fees and spreads, on Polymarket or Kalshi. The Kalshi pilot in arXiv 2607.06166 reports no numbers in its abstract.
- A search snippet says a 2025 paper found RLVR accuracy optimization *worsened* trading returns (best-accuracy agent −14.8% return). I could not identify or verify the source.
- Results of FRI's October 2026 ForecastBench "rematch" (fresh superforecaster round) were not yet published or findable.

## What do the major benchmarks show (latest numbers)?

### Takeaway
On ForecastBench, the best AI systems reached a statistical tie with the 2024 superforecaster baseline in July 2026, ahead of FRI's projected November 2026 parity date. On Metaculus FutureEval, the Pro lead over bots collapsed from −20 points per question (Q2 2025) to −1.25 (Spring 2026, not significant) and −0.26 (Summer 2026, not significant). Market-based benchmarks (Prophet Arena, TimeSeek, KalshiBench) show near-market Brier but no reliable outperformance, and miscalibration is common.

### Cited Findings
**ForecastBench (Karger et al., FRI; ICLR 2025)**
- Design: rounds of 500 questions, 250 "market" questions (Manifold, Metaculus, Polymarket, Infer/RAND) and 250 "dataset" questions (ACLED, DBnomics, FRED, Wikipedia, yfinance). Market-question difficulty is the market's own Brier on the due date. Scoring is a difficulty-adjusted Brier / "Brier Index". — [Copier Line](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually); [FRI Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)
- **Oct 2025:** the median public forecaster fell from #2 to #22 on the leaderboard, so LLMs now beat the general public. Best LLM GPT-4.5 scored 0.101 vs superforecasters 0.081 (difficulty-adjusted Brier). — [11-analyses synthesis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Jan 29, 2026 (FRI):** superforecasters ranked #1, ahead of state-of-the-art LLMs by **0.017** difficulty-adjusted Brier, which FRI says equals about one year of LLM progress. Best LLMs: xAI Grok 4.20 Preview at **0.102**, tied with Cassi's `ensemble_2_crowdadj` (o3 + GPT-5). Claude 3.5 Sonnet (Oct 2024) scored 0.117, an improvement of about **0.015 Brier per year**. Projected parity was **Nov 2026 (95% CI Jan 2026–Nov 2027)**: dataset questions Jun 2026, market questions Aug 2026. — [FRI, "LLMs Are Closing the Gap"](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human). The synthesis gives a different CI (Dec 2025–Jan 2028), probably from an earlier FRI estimate. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Jul 6, 2026 snapshot:** the superforecaster median was still #1 overall (Brier Index 69.3). Lightning Rod's Foresight-v3 was #4 at 67.7. Gemini, Cassi and Foresight-v3 were inside the superforecasters' CI. — [Lightning Rod blog, Jul 8, 2026](https://blog.lightningrod.ai/p/ai-reaches-human-superforecaster-range)
- **Jul 16, 2026 (FRI): "AI models have likely reached parity."** One-sided bootstrap p-values vs equal accuracy with superforecasters: Cassi AI **0.41**, xAI submissions 0.16 and 0.15, Google DeepMind 0.14. On the preliminary dataset-question leaderboard, 17 submissions rank above superforecasters, with Torchcast submissions on top. On market questions, Cassi and one anonymous ensemble rank above the superforecaster median, the first models to do so. FRI frames the result as parity, not outperformance. The superforecaster baseline was last collected in 2024. — [FRI Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity). FRI's X thread gives 95% CIs of 67.9–70.5 for the leading system and 67.3–70.5 for superforecasters, and announces an October 2026 "rematch" with superforecasters returning. — [FRI on X](https://x.com/Research_FRI/status/2102411966103093386)
- Index caveat: the published superforecaster Brier Index fell 4.7 points in four months while their adjusted score stayed the same, because the rescaling anchor rose. — [Copier Line](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- Critiques (Good Judgment, Jul 24, 2026): AI systems that keep improving are compared with a "frozen July 2024 snapshot" of superforecasters who worked largely alone with little updating. Some "market" questions are "dataset questions in market clothing", for example World Cup goals or July 2026 temperature. The question mix is data-resolvable rather than novel geopolitics. — [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/not-so-fast). FRI acknowledged that the questions newer models forecast do not overlap with the ones superforecasters forecast. — [Good Judgment (search summary)](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/the-hype-and-the-evidence-about-ai)
- Conflict: one Jul 2026 arXiv paper says the "Belief-Logit-Forecaster (BLF) of Murphy (2026)… currently lead[s] ForecastBench". This differs from FRI naming Cassi as top, perhaps because they refer to different leaderboards or dates. — [arXiv 2607.03015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03015)

**Metaculus AI Benchmarking → FutureEval (bots vs 10 Pro forecasters, head-to-head peer score per question; negative = Pros ahead)**
- Q3 2024: −11.3 (95% CI −21.8 to −0.7, p = 0.036). Q4 2024: −8.9 (p = 0.079). Q1 2025: −17.7 (p = 0.001). Q2 2025: −20.03 (CI −28.6 to −11.4, p = 0.00001; 54 bots). — [FutureEval Spring results](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is); [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Spring 2026 (Jan 7–Apr 15, 2026; post dated Sep 9, 2026):** 173 bots, 297 scored questions, $50k prizes. Bot team vs Pros: **−1.25 (CI −4.87 to +2.37; one-sided p = 0.247; 99 questions)**. Pros won about 64% of questions. By type: binary −2.51 (63 questions), multiple-choice −5.02 (9), **numeric +3.47 (21)**. Ranked as individuals, Pros took 9 of the top 10 places. - Spring 2026 "Metac" baseline bots (same prompt, different LLMs, all with AskNews): GPT-5.1-high 11.32, Claude 4.5 Sonnet-high 8.94, GPT-5.2-high 8.62, o3 8.46, DeepSeek-3.2-reasoning 7.22, Grok-4.1-fast-high 6.93. The best baseline placed 18th of 173. Earlier, o3 placed 2nd of 96 (Q2 2025) and the best baseline 14th of 134 (Fall 2025), so scaffolded bots are pulling ahead of plain prompting. — [FutureEval Spring](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is)
- **Summer 2026 (preliminary analysis, merged Oct 15, 2026):** 282 bots (81 Metac bots), 326 scored questions. Bot team vs Pros **−0.26 (CI −6.47 to +5.96; two-sided p = 0.94)** on 97 scored Pro questions. Pros won 60 questions and bots 37. — [Metaculus aib-analysis PR #33](https://github.com/Metaculus/aib-analysis/pull/33)
- FutureEval leaderboard (May 2026): the best individual model trails Pros by about 16 peer points. A naive linear extrapolation has bots passing Pros around **June 2027**. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- Metaculus Cup (open human tournament): Mantic placed 8th of 549 in Summer 2025, the first bot in the top 10, and 4th in Fall 2025, also beating the weighted human aggregate. In Summer 2026 (Sep 5, 2026), bots took 1st, 2nd and 5th. "laertes" finished first and Mantic ahead of every human; Mantic claims it beat all 676 humans. Cup scoring rewards coverage, earliness and frequent updates, which favours bots. — [The Star/Reuters, Sep 18, 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/18/ai-startup-mantic-raises-25-million-for-superhuman-forecasting); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/ai-takes-1st-2nd-and-5th-places-at-metaculus-cup/tldr); [explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/ai-beats-forecasters-metaculus-cup-coverage-2026); [Thinking Machines/Mantic](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/)

**Prophet Arena** (UChicago, Haifeng Xu group; ICLR 2026): a live benchmark on Kalshi events, adding about 20 new events per day and evaluating 23 LLMs. It scores Brier, ECE and simulated betting return against the market. Headline numbers are in the section above (GPT-5R 0.184 vs market 0.187; returns below break-even). — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)

**KalshiBench** (Lukas Nel, arXiv Dec 2025): 300 Kalshi questions in 13 categories, resolving after model training cutoffs. Five frontier models reached only 64–69% accuracy. The best-calibrated, Claude Opus 4.5, had ECE 0.120, while GPT-5.2-XHigh had ECE 0.395 at similar accuracy. "Reasoning enhancements appeared to degrade calibration." These numbers come from search snippets, not the full text, and later papers cite other dataset sizes (v2: 1,189 resolved questions). — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030); [arXiv 2605.30802](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30802v1)

**FutureX** (Aug 2025; ICLR 2026): a live, contamination-free benchmark of about 500 events per week from 195 websites across 11 domains, evaluating 25 models and agents (including Deep Research agents). Performance falls steadily across four difficulty tiers (Basic → Super Agent). Observed failures include susceptibility to fake web pages, weak real-time search, and poor results on highly volatile targets. — [arXiv 2508.11987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11987)

**TimeSeek** (Apr 2026): see the section above. It is the cleanest "LLM vs contemporaneous Kalshi price" study, testing at five points in each market's life.

### Inferences
- The gap is closing at roughly 0.015 difficulty-adjusted Brier per year on ForecastBench. On Metaculus it went from about −20 to about 0 in roughly one year (Q2 2025 → Summer 2026). Bots beating Pros or superforecasters by a statistically significant margin is plausible in 2027, but it had not been shown as of Nov 2026.
- "Superforecaster parity" ≠ "beats markets". On ForecastBench market questions, superforecasters themselves only narrowly beat a stale shown price (uncorrected p = 0.017), and liquid markets are tougher baselines still.

### Gaps
- Live Prophet Arena and FutureX leaderboards as of Nov 2026 were not retrieved. Their numbers above date from Oct 2025 or earlier.
- No per-question-type breakdown was available for Summer 2026 FutureEval.
- No "PredictionArena"-style market eval was investigated in depth (out of scope; another researcher covers live arenas). Prediction Arena (arXiv 2604.07355) and Foresight Arena (arXiv 2605.00420) exist.

## Key papers (Halawi, Schoenegger, leakage, fine-tuning/RL)

### Takeaway
Early work (2024) put retrieval-augmented LLMs near, but below, human crowds (Halawi: 0.179 vs 0.149) and found LLM ensembles about equal to a human crowd (Schoenegger). Leakage research shows many backtested "superhuman" claims are inflated. RL fine-tuning on resolved questions gives real but modest gains, roughly frontier-level from smaller or open models, and decorrelated errors that help ensembles.

### Cited Findings
**Halawi, Zhang, Yueh-Han & Steinhardt (2024), "Approaching Human-Level Forecasting"**
- Test set of 914 binary questions (Metaculus, GJOpen, INFER, Polymarket, Manifold) opened after the model's knowledge cutoff. System Brier **0.179 vs human crowd 0.149**. Accuracy 71.5% vs 77.0%. Weighting the crowd 4× with the system gave **0.146**, slightly better than the crowd alone. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)
- Ablations: full system 0.179, no fine-tuning 0.186, no fine-tuning and no retrieval 0.206, zero-shot GPT-4 0.208. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)
- Fine-tuning: generated 73,632 reasonings and kept the 13,253 that beat the crowd. Targets were averaged with the crowd, and pairs more than 0.15 from the crowd were dropped to limit overconfidence. The final forecast is a trimmed mean of 6 forecasts, and trimmed mean was the best of 5 aggregators tested. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)
- Selective edge: when the crowd was uncertain (0.3–0.7; 51% of forecasts) the system scored 0.238 vs crowd 0.240. With three favourable conditions combined (22% of forecasts), it scored 0.240 vs 0.247. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)

**Schoenegger et al.**
- "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd" (Science Advances): an ensemble of 12 LLMs on 31 binary questions was statistically indistinguishable from a crowd of 925 human tournament forecasters, and beat a no-information baseline. LLMs showed an acquiescence effect (bias toward "yes") and favoured round numbers. In Study 2, GPT-4 and Claude 2 improved 17–28% when shown the human median, but **simply averaging human and machine forecasts was more accurate**. — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379)
- "AI-Augmented Predictions" (N = 991): LLM assistants improved human forecasting accuracy by **24–28%** vs control (published version; preprint said about 23%). A deliberately noisy, overconfident assistant also helped (about 29% vs about 41% for the superforecasting assistant, after excluding one outlier question). — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/127059); [arXiv 2402.07862](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.07862)

**Look-ahead / leakage bias in backtesting**
- Paleka, Goel, Geiping & Tramèr, "Pitfalls in Evaluating LM Forecasters" (ICLR 2026): leakage comes from choosing questions with knowledge of how they turned out, wrong document timestamps, retrievers trained on post-cutoff data, and search ranking that reflects present-day relevance. Example: a search restricted to Jan 2024 surfaced an article about the Oct 2024 Nobel Peace Prize. The authors warn that a model's knowledge cutoff is not a reliable boundary. — [arXiv 2506.00723](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)
- El Lahib et al. (UCSD, ACL 2026 short), audit of Google `before:` and DuckDuckGo date filters on 393 resolved questions: about **71%** of questions returned at least one page with major post-cutoff information, and **41%** (Google) returned a page that directly revealed the answer. Leakage produced misleading **Brier 0.10 vs 0.24**. They recommend frozen, timestamped web snapshots. — [arXiv 2602.00758](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00758v1)
- Backtested parity claims: AIA Forecaster (backtest Brier 0.108 vs superforecaster median 0.111) acknowledges leakage concerns. Its LLM judge estimated about 1.65% of search results were leaky, and removing flagged items moved Brier by only +0.17%. Phan et al. reported 0.0934 vs human crowd 0.0952, but only after Platt scaling that was not applied to the crowd, and Halawi's informal replication failed. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say); [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)
- Knowledge staleness: Daily Oracle found performance degrades as training data ages (21.5% for true/false and 11.3% for multiple-choice over four years). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)

**Fine-tuning / RL on resolved outcomes**
- Turtel et al. (Lightning Rod), see the overall section: Brier-reward RL plus a ReMax ensemble gives a small hypothetical Polymarket profit, but Polymarket is still more accurate. — [arXiv 2505.17989v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17989v3)
- Mantic × Thinking Machines (Mar 19, 2026): RL on gpt-oss-120b (GRPO-style policy gradient, Brier reward, ~10k binary "will X happen by date" questions from Aug 2024–Dec 2025, all after the model's cutoff). Tested on the Metaculus Q2 2025 AI benchmark, it raised the Metaculus baseline score from **38.6 to 45.8** with Mantic's research and tools, marginally above Gemini 3 Pro. Model-only gain was about 3 points. The tuned model's predictions are **decorrelated** from frontier models. In the optimal 5-sample ensemble it gets 40% of the weight, with Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5 and Grok 4 at 20% each. Grok 4 is the least replaceable ensemble member. — [Thinking Machines](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/)
- Afifi et al., "Do Your Own Research" (Oct 1, 2026; NeurIPS 2026 workshop): agentic RL (single-epoch GRPO, Brier reward) on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with search, page-reading and financial time-series tools, using 2,100+ resolved Polymarket questions with layered leak filtering. Soft-Brier **0.254 vs Claude Opus 4.5 0.256** (n = 265) at about 5% of the cost. Calibration improved 30–40%, and search attempts per rollout fell from 3.8 to 2.25. The margin was widest on questions the crowd had not decided. — [arXiv 2610.01955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01955)
- Synthesis: open-weight fine-tunes (Turtel, Lightning Rod Foresight-32B, OpenForecaster) reach the parity of the previous generation of closed models, not the current frontier. Lightning Rod's Foresight-v3 ranked #4 on ForecastBench (Jul 2026) and runs on a single GPU. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say); [Lightning Rod](https://blog.lightningrod.ai/p/ai-reaches-human-superforecaster-range)

### Inferences
- Any backtest of an LLM trading strategy using live web search with date filters is likely to overstate edge badly (0.10 vs 0.24 Brier in one audit). Only forward (paper or live) testing, or frozen snapshots, are credible.
- The main practical value of RL fine-tuning may be **decorrelation for ensembles** and lower cost rather than a big jump in standalone accuracy.

### Gaps
- Phan et al. and OpenForecaster primary sources were not retrieved; the figures come via the 11-analyses synthesis.
- Turtel et al.'s base model and exact Brier figures were not verified here.

## Where do LLMs do relatively better vs worse?

### Takeaway
LLMs are relatively strongest early in a market's life, at long horizons, on uncertain (near-50%) questions, and on data-rich or numeric questions. They are weakest near resolution, on fast-breaking news, on strong-consensus markets, and (in some studies) in politics and finance. There is no direct evidence on illiquid or low-attention markets.

### Cited Findings
- **Horizon:** in Prophet Arena, several frontier LLMs beat the market at longer horizons, while "markets adjust faster near resolution and achieve the best short-term accuracy". — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2). In TimeSeek, four models beat the market at Open+1 (Claude Opus 4.5 BSS +0.167), and all were far worse at Close-1 (−0.73 to −1.74). — [arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220)
- **Market uncertainty:** on TimeSeek toss-up markets, BSS was Claude +0.301, GPT-5.2 +0.217, Kimi-k2.5 +0.179. Easy (near-certain) markets were negative for all models (−0.69 to −1.68). When Claude strongly disagreed with the market, it was right 80.9% of the time on toss-ups (n = 68) but only 19.8% on easy markets (n = 81). — [arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220). Turtel: about 20% hypothetical ROI on low-market-confidence questions vs about 10% overall, and no edge where the market was confident. — [arXiv 2505.17989v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17989v3). Halawi: the system matched the crowd only when the crowd was at 0.3–0.7. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1). On ForecastBench, forecasters' edge over the market is largest where the market is least certain, and they close about two-thirds of the gap to the market on hard questions (pass-through 0.674). — [Copier Line](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- **Category:** in TimeSeek with search, Claude scored +0.294 in Sports and +0.163 in Macro, and every model was negative in Politics and Financial. Search helped all models in Macro (average +0.96) and Sports (+0.39) but hurt most in Politics. — [arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220). In Prophet Arena, Climate/Weather and Politics showed low recall and frequent false recall. — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)
- **Question type (Metaculus Spring 2026):** bots beat Pros on numeric (+3.47) and lost on binary (−2.51) and multiple-choice (−5.02). Samples were small. — [FutureEval Spring](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is)
- **Data-rich questions:** FRI projected parity on dataset questions (Jun 2026) before market questions (Aug 2026). By Jul 2026, 17 submissions beat superforecasters on the preliminary dataset leaderboard. — [FRI Jan 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human); [FRI Jul 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity). Good Judgment argues ForecastBench skews toward data-resolvable questions where AI has structural advantages, and says it has not seen LLMs match superforecasters in novel, sparse-data client work (small sample). — [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/not-so-fast)
- **Hard tasks and volatility:** FutureX performance drops steadily with task difficulty, and agents do poorly on highly volatile targets. — [arXiv 2508.11987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11987). In Afifi et al., the RL-trained agent's margin was widest on questions the crowd had not decided. — [arXiv 2610.01955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01955)

### Inferences
- The best opportunities to trade are **new or long-dated markets that are priced near 50%**, especially in sports and macro. LLMs should be avoided or deferred to the market near resolution and on markets that are already priced at extreme probabilities.
- Better results in "low-confidence" markets may partly reflect fewer informed traders, so it may overlap with illiquidity. That is untested.

### Gaps
- No study I found isolates **liquidity or attention** (volume, open interest) as a factor in LLM-vs-market performance. TimeSeek reports a volume range ($5.6k–$41.3M, median $35k) but did not analyze it.
- Evidence on rapidly breaking news is indirect (near-resolution underperformance, FutureX's weak real-time search).

## Which techniques measurably improve LLM forecasting?

### Takeaway
The techniques with the strongest quantified support are, in rough order: (1) a strong reasoning model at high reasoning effort; (2) agentic, iterative news search from several providers; (3) ensembling 5–10+ samples or models with a median or mean; (4) post-hoc Platt scaling / extremizing, fitted per model; (5) combining with, rather than replacing, the market or crowd price; (6) RL fine-tuning for decorrelated ensemble members. Prompt optimization and elaborate multi-persona LLM merging have weak or negative live evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Model and reasoning effort:** frontier model choice was the largest differentiator in early Metaculus quarters. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). High reasoning beat standard in 8 of 8 Metac pairs (p = 0.004), for example GPT-5.1 at 11.3 vs 4.56. Every top-10 Spring 2026 respondent used GPT-5.2+ in their final ensemble. — [FutureEval Spring](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is). Counterpoint: KalshiBench found reasoning enhancements degraded calibration (GPT-5.2-XHigh ECE 0.395). — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)
- **Retrieval / agentic search:** AIA on FB-7-21: no search 0.1230, non-agentic search 0.1174, agentic search 0.1140. In the live test, no search scored 0.3609, worse than always saying 50%, vs 0.1002 with search. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1). TimeSeek: search improved pooled BSS for all 10 models (+0.14 to +0.59) but hurt in 6 of 50 model-checkpoint cells, mostly Politics. — [arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220). Halawi: no retrieval and no fine-tuning scored 0.206 vs 0.186 without fine-tuning only. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1). In the Fall 2025 survey, the number of research sources correlated with score (r = 0.42, p = 0.006). No single search provider was consistently better across three seasons, but **using 2–3 providers** predicted performance. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Ensembling and aggregation:** AIA: single forecast 0.1182 vs mean of 10 at 0.1140, median 0.1138, trimmed mean 0.1142, so the aggregator barely matters. Most of the gain comes from 1 → 5 samples, with modest gains up to 15. An agentic "supervisor" that reconciles disagreements reached 0.1125, against 0.1168 for a non-agentic supervisor and 0.1191 for best-of-k. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1). 86% of Fall 2025 winners aggregated multiple forecasts, and winners averaged about 28 LLM calls per question vs 7 (p = 0.022). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). Ensembling **decorrelated** models matters: GPT-5 and Gemini 3 Pro are highly correlated, while Grok 4 and a fine-tuned gpt-oss-120b add the most. — [Thinking Machines/Mantic](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/)
- **Calibration post-processing:** in AIA, Platt scaling with a fixed coefficient of √3 (≈1.73) moved 0.1140 to **0.1076**. Log-odds extremization gave 0.1085, isotonic 0.1097, OLS 0.1119. The superforecaster-optimal Platt coefficient was 1.72 and AIA's own 2.27. Prompting to reduce hedging "was largely ineffective", and calibration helps only when the forecast is already on the correct side of 0.5. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1). Metaculus (May 2026) tested 5 methods on Q1+Q2 bot forecasts: **Platt scaling won**, with Brier improvement of 0.016 on binary (p = 0.00052) and 0.005 on multiple-choice. Phan et al.: 0.0999 → 0.0934 with Platt. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). Note the opposite lever: **capping or clipping extreme predictions** correlated with winning (r = +0.48, p = 0.005), which protects against catastrophic log-score losses. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). Cassi uses no post-forecast calibration, saying current LLMs are already broadly calibrated. — [FRI Jan 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)
- **Base rates and reference classes:** explicit base rates correlated with winning (r = +0.38, p = 0.032). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). Builders recommend Tetlock-style outside/inside view and "consider the opposite" prompts. — [Metaculus bot advice](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/)
- **Anchoring on the market or crowd price:**
  - AIA, ForecastBench market subset: no search and no price 0.116; no search with price 0.103; **price alone 0.096**; agentic search without price 0.085; agentic search with price **0.075**. A model given the price but no research does *worse than the price alone*. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)
  - Cassi's "crowd adjustment" has an LLM review its forecast when it differs a lot from the market price, which improved its score by nearly 0.01 Brier. — [FRI Jan 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)
  - Combining the forecast with the price works better than showing the price to the LLM: Halawi's 4× crowd-weighted average gave 0.146 vs crowd 0.149, Schoenegger found averaging beats showing models the human median, and AIA's market+LLM ensemble gave 0.106 vs market 0.111. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1); [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379); [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)
  - Using the Metaculus community prediction as a *training target* underperformed live. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Scaffolding overall:** top bots beat their own unscaffolded baseline by 5–11 points per question, about 9 months of base-model progress (~0.9 points per month). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Fine-tuning / RL:** Halawi fine-tuning 0.186 → 0.179. Mantic RL 38.6 → 45.8 Metaculus baseline score. Afifi RL lets a 3B-active model match Claude Opus 4.5. — sources above
- **Numeric questions:** Mantic has the LLM parameterize a mixture of scenarios (components, parameters and weights) rather than state quantiles directly. — [Thinking Machines](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/). One bot maker had success asking for mean and SD instead of percentiles. — [Metaculus bot advice](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/)
- **Prompt optimization:** automated prompt engineering gave large gains on GPT-4.1-nano (~18 points) but none on DeepSeek-R1, and the **live replication failed** (optimized prompt +2.76 per question vs baseline +4.71). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Consistency:** logical-consistency scores (negation, paraphrase, conditional) correlate with accuracy, but an "ArbitrageForecaster" optimized for consistency did not improve actual forecasting. — [Paleka et al., arXiv 2412.18544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18544)
- **Cost:** top-15 Fall 2025 winners spent about $1.40 per question vs about $0.50 for others. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)

### Inferences
- A sensible baseline pipeline:
  1. Two or three search providers in an iterative agentic loop, with frozen snapshots for backtests.
  2. 5–10 samples from 2–4 *decorrelated* reasoning models at high effort.
  3. Median or mean aggregation, optionally with an agentic supervisor.
  4. Platt scaling with a coefficient of about 1.5–2.3, fitted on forward data per model.
  5. Finally, a weighted blend with the live market price, with the weight learned out of sample (AIA's learned weight on the liquid market was about 0.67).
- Extremizing and clipping are both valid, applied to different failure modes: extremize to fix systematic hedging, clip to cap tail-risk losses. Whether each helps depends on the model; reasoning models may already be overconfident (KalshiBench).

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence was found on self-critique or debate as standalone techniques. The anecdotal reports conflict: AIA's supervisor helped, while Metaculus builders said "LLM merges distributions" and persona-merging hurt.
- No quantified study was found of Platt or extremizing applied to *trading* returns against market prices, as opposed to Brier.

## Known failure modes

### Takeaway
The main documented failures are: hedging toward 50% (RLHF conservatism) for some models and overconfidence for some reasoning models; copying the market price instead of adding information; leaky backtests; misreading resolution status or criteria; stale knowledge; and directional or framing biases.

### Cited Findings
- **Hedging toward 0.5:** a true probability of about 0.85 is often reported as about 0.6, and near-certain events get 0.95. AIA attributes this to RLHF and says prompting doesn't fix it. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1). Prophet Arena: LLM forecasts sit closer to 0.5 than market prices even when markets are near-certain. — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)
- **Overconfidence and miscalibration:** GPT-5.2-XHigh ECE 0.395, Claude Opus 4.5 ECE 0.120, and reasoning appeared to worsen calibration. — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030)
- **Market copying:** GPT-4.5 correlated 0.994 with the shown price. — [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/not-so-fast). Among top-30 ForecastBench entries, 41–67% of forecasts were within 0.5 percentage points of the shown market price. — [Copier Line](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- **Acquiescence and round numbers:** LLMs lean toward "yes" and round numbers. — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379). Directional (valence) bias: all 16 models tested showed significant directional bias, 14 of them optimistic (OptimismBench preprint, peer review unverified). — [HF papers 2607.26981](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26981.md)
- **Logical inconsistency:** forecasts violate negation, paraphrase and conditional coherence. — [arXiv 2412.18544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18544)
- **Resolution and status confusion:** bots treat unresolved questions as resolved and output catastrophic 99% forecasts. Unit bugs and missed question formats cost one maker more than 1,000 points (about $500) on three questions, and another case cost about 150 points. — [Metaculus bot advice](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/); [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- **Approximate recall and date errors:** for example, a Billboard chart date mismatched by two days. Sources can confound as well as clarify. — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)
- **Slow aggregation near resolution:** see the horizon findings above. — [arXiv 2510.17638v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2); [arXiv 2604.04220](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04220)
- **Stale knowledge** (Daily Oracle degradation) and **fake or low-quality web pages** (FutureX). — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say); [arXiv 2508.11987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11987)
- **Run-to-run noise:** single runs are noisy and worse in expectation than an average of several runs. Naive LLM aggregation overweights outliers. — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1). GPT-5's temperature could not be adjusted, which increased variance. — [Metaculus bot advice](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/)
- **Leaky evaluation:** see the leakage section (71% / 41% leak rates; 0.10 vs 0.24 Brier). — [arXiv 2602.00758](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00758v1)

### Inferences
- In a trading agent, hedging toward 50% quietly leads to buying cheap longshots and fading favourites. That is the classic favourite–longshot pattern, and it would likely lose money against extreme-priced markets, consistent with TimeSeek's results on easy markets.

### Gaps
- No direct study was found of **sycophancy toward question framing** in forecasting, for example whether "Will X happen?" and "Will X fail to happen?" get different answers beyond negation-consistency checks.

## What do top AIB/ForecastBench bot builders say worked?

### Takeaway
Builders agree that research quality, the frontier reasoning model, ensembling or median-of-many, and robust edge-case handling matter most. Simple, clean pipelines beat clever architectures. Backtests on resolved questions are distrusted because of leakage.

### Cited Findings
- From the Fall 2025 advice (Metaculus, Mar 2026):
  - Use the strongest reasoning model you can afford.
  - External search is mandatory, and it should be iterative (query → search → find gaps → search again).
  - Pull in existing forecasts from Metaculus and other sites.
  - Run 5+ forecasts and take the median; cutting to 1 forecast hurt.
  - Clipping and extremization are underused.
  - Use base rates, inside/outside view, and code execution.
  - "Clear prompting usually beats clever architecture." Several makers found "improving" the template made it worse.
  - Don't backtest on resolved questions (leakage); use minibench.
  - Guard against treating open questions as resolved.
  - What didn't work: cheap multi-persona pipelines feeding a weak final merge, and LLM-merged distributions.

  — [Metaculus bot advice](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43357/bot-advice-fall-2025/)
- Fall 2025 survey (39 bot makers): 34 of 39 used a frontier model. Research sources r = 0.42, capping r = +0.48, base rates r = +0.38, LLM calls per question 28 vs 7, and cost per question $1.40 vs $0.50. — [11-analyses](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)
- Spring 2026 survey (58 responses): themes were simplicity over complexity and research quality as the bottleneck. 9 of the top-10 respondents used GPT-5.4, whose use correlated 0.42 with performance, but no survey result survived correction for 33 tests. 7 of the top 10 makers were hobbyists. — [FutureEval Spring](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is)
- Cassi (top ForecastBench, Jul 2026): an ensemble of models, with another LLM analyzing the reasoning to produce the final forecast. A crowd/market adjustment step was worth about 0.01 Brier, and there is no post-hoc calibration. — [FRI Jan 2026](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human); [FRI Jul 2026 (search summary)](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)
- Mantic: deep-research agents summarize context into the prompt, then the LLM writes chain-of-thought and outputs a scenario mixture model. Subtasks are routed across models from several labs, and it entered the Metaculus Cup without fine-tuning in 2025. — [Thinking Machines](https://www.thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/); [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/18/ai-startup-mantic-raises-25-million-for-superhuman-forecasting)
- AIA Forecaster (Bridgewater): agentic search, multiple independent agents, a supervisor that reconciles disagreements, then Platt scaling (√3). — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)

### Inferences
- The winning bots' recipes are now common knowledge, so differentiation is moving to research quality, model diversity and decorrelation, operational reliability, and (for trading) the market-blending and bet-sizing layer.

### Gaps
- Detailed write-ups from the specific Spring and Summer 2026 winners (GreeneiBot2, Preseen, SynapseSeer, laertes) were not found or retrieved.
