# Legal, Regulatory, Tax, and Non-Market Risks for a US Individual Running a Prediction-Market Trading Bot (as of 2026-11-16)

> Scope note: informational research only, not legal or tax advice. The law here changed month to month through 2026. Several items are pending (SCOTUS cert, Fourth Circuit, CFTC rules with comments due 2026-11-18, the FAIR BET Act). Many sources are law-firm alerts, trade press, or affiliate blogs. Where a claim rests on one secondary source, it is flagged. Before relying on a specific date or posture, check the primary dockets, the Federal Register, and the platform rulebooks.

## 1. Federal status: CFTC regulation of event contracts (late 2026)

### Takeaway
At the federal level, conditions in late 2026 are the most permissive they have ever been. Kalshi won its election-contract case and the CFTC dropped its appeal (May 2025). Under Chairman Michael Selig (in office since Dec 2025), the CFTC withdrew the 2024 ban proposal (Feb 2026). It then proposed (a) replacing categorical event-contract bans with case-by-case "public interest" reviews (June 2026) and (b) writing event contracts, including sports and politics, into the statutory "swap" definition (Oct 2026). It also issued an interim rule carving state-licensed casino and sportsbook products out of that definition. No federal statute has been enacted. The CFTC is aggressively defending its jurisdiction against states, and at the same time it is making insider trading on prediction markets its top enforcement priority.

### Inferences
- For a small retail bot trading on a CFTC-registered DCM (Kalshi, Polymarket US, or Robinhood's Kalshi feed), federal regulatory risk to the user is low. The live federal risk is enforcement for manipulation or insider trading (Section 4), not for participating.
- The Oct 2026 NPRM is not final. Until it is, the "are sports contracts swaps?" question stays with the courts. A change of administration or CFTC leadership could reverse the permissive posture, since much of it rests on withdrawals and proposals rather than final rules.
- The June 2026 Rule 40.11 proposal implies some contract types (injury, officiating, single-play props) may be delisted or never listed. A bot should handle markets disappearing.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the June 2026 Rule 40.11 NPRM has been finalized, or its comment deadline.
- Could not confirm whether the D.C. Circuit formally granted the CFTC's May 2025 dismissal motion (widely reported as ending the case).
- One unverified report said the CFTC warned Kalshi of possible charges over new contract notices. No second source was found.
- Did not verify Robinhood's current exchange arrangements (for example, whether it runs its own DCM versus routing to Kalshi) or Coinbase/DraftKings/FanDuel event-contract offerings.

## 2. State-level fights: cease-and-desists, lawsuits, appellate rulings, and what they mean for an individual user

### Takeaway
The federal appeals courts are now split on sports contracts:
- The Third Circuit (NJ, Apr 2026) held them to be swaps preempting state law.
- The Ninth (NV, Aug 2026) and Sixth (OH/TN, Sept 2026) Circuits held they are not swaps and states can regulate them.
- The Fourth Circuit (MD) is pending.

New Jersey's Sept 2026 cert petition makes Supreme Court review likely, but a decision is not expected before early 2027. Sports contracts are currently unavailable, or available only on some platforms, in several states (NV, MI, UT, MA, WA, plus Polymarket-specific blocks). In every action found, the targets were platforms (civil suits, fines, and Arizona's misdemeanor charges against Kalshi entities), not individual users. The main user-level risk is losing access, or having positions force-closed or unwound, rather than prosecution.

  - The ruling covered Kalshi, Robinhood Derivatives, and Crypto.com/Nadex. It is binding Ninth Circuit precedent, though it is not a final merits judgment.

### Inferences
- User-level legal risk from state gambling laws looks low in practice. No reported case targets a retail trader. The realistic harms are:
  - Geofencing: the bot's account loses access to sports markets mid-season.
  - Forced unwinds, as in Michigan in June–July 2026, at prices you did not choose.
  - Being caught between a state order and a CFTC order.
- Non-sports contracts (elections, economics, weather) have been far less contested, though Nevada, Connecticut, and Arizona have also challenged election contracts. A bot focused on economic or weather markets has less exposure.
- Treat state availability as dynamic. The bot should read the platform's per-state market eligibility rather than hard-coding it.
- If SCOTUS takes the case and rules for the states (2027 at the earliest), sports contracts could become state-licensed products and disappear from DCMs in many states.

### Gaps
- No source addressed whether any state's gambling statute criminalizes the *player* (as opposed to the operator) for trading DCM event contracts. Some states historically penalize online gambling participants, so this warrants a lawyer's view in the user's home state.
- The CBS tracker contradicts itself on Massachusetts and Utah, and no Robinhood- or Crypto.com-specific state list was found.
- The current status of the Arizona criminal case (beyond "permanently blocked, appeals not exhausted") and the Michigan TRO after July 2026 was not confirmed.

## 3. Polymarket international: US geoblock, VPN consequences, 2022 settlement, and the 2024–2025 investigations

### Takeaway
The global, crypto-native Polymarket.com remains restricted for US persons as of Nov 2026. It is listed as "close-only": you can exit existing positions but not open new ones. Its terms (reported as §2.1.4) prohibit VPNs and proxies. US residents' lawful route is Polymarket US, a KYC'd, intermediated DCM. The 2022 CFTC order ($1.4M) is what forced the US block. The DOJ/CFTC probes that followed the Nov 2024 FBI raid (which examined US users bypassing the block via VPN) were reportedly closed in July 2025 without charges. Using a VPN today risks account suspension with funds held, and it creates evidence of deliberate evasion at a time the CFTC is hunting insider trading and reportedly tracking VPN use.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 order:** in January 2022 the CFTC ordered Blockratize Inc. (d/b/a Polymarket) to pay a $1.4M civil penalty for offering off-exchange binary options/swaps without DCM or SEF registration.
  - Polymarket had to wind down non-compliant markets. The penalty was reduced for cooperation, and there was no admission or denial of wrongdoing.
  - The order did not bar future DCM or SEF registration.
  - Sources: [The Block (2022)](https://www.theblock.co/linked/129047/crypto-prediction-market-platform-polymarket-settles-with-cftc-for-1-4-million); [DLA Piper (Jan 2022)](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2022/1/cftc-settles-enforcement-action-against-defi-platform-polymarket)
- **2024–2025 probes:** the FBI raided CEO Shayne Coplan's home in Nov 2024. Bloomberg reported on 2025-07-15 that the DOJ and CFTC had formally notified Polymarket their investigations had ended.
  - The probes examined whether Polymarket accepted wagers from US users "using virtual private networks or other means to bypass the company's controls."
  - The agencies declined comment. "Cleared of wrongdoing" framing in some outlets is not backed by an official statement.
  - Sources: [The Block (2025-07-15)](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-07-15-us-justice-department-and-cftc-close-probes-into-polymarket-bloomberg-362703); [Bloomberg (2025-07-15)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-15/polymarket-probe-ended-by-doj-in-win-for-crypto-bets-under-trump)
- **Current geoblock:** as of a 2026-11-08 compilation, the US (and US Minor Outlying Islands) is "close-only" on both the website and the API. That source attributes the list to Polymarket's developer geoblock docs. ## 4. Platform rules on automated trading, manipulation, wash trading, spoofing, and insider trading

### Takeaway
Kalshi explicitly allows automated trading through its official API under a Developer Agreement. API use is limited to your own trading, you may not redistribute data, Kalshi may audit, and rate limits apply. Polymarket US has its own exchange rulebook and API. The real legal exposure for a bot is market-conduct law, which applies to individual traders under the CEA:
- wash trading, self-matching, and pre-arranged trades (CEA §4c(a), Reg 1.38)
- spoofing and disruptive trading (CEA §4c(a)(5))
- manipulation and misappropriation-based insider trading (CEA §6(c)(1), Rule 180.1)

The CFTC's Feb 2026 advisory and 2026 enforcement priorities make insider trading the top priority. Platforms now run surveillance, employer disclosure, and whistleblower tools, and penalties include multi-year bans and fines of 5–10x the trade size.

### Cited Findings
**Kalshi bot and API rules**
- Using Kalshi's API constitutes acceptance of the Developer Agreement. Its terms:
  - Use is limited to facilitating the member's own trading on the exchange.
  - Collecting, caching, or aggregating API data is barred except for your own trading, and data may not be shared without written consent.
  - The user must be an exchange Member and comply with the Rulebook.
  - Kalshi may change rate limits without notice and audit API use on notice.
  - Termination ends API licenses immediately.
  - Source: [Kalshi Developer Agreement](https://kalshi-public-docs.s3.amazonaws.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf)
- Rate limits are token-based. A throttled request returns HTTP 429, and a 25-order batch is rejected whole if tokens are insufficient. — [Kalshi docs: rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)
- **Prohibited conduct:** Kalshi lists wash trades, insider trading, spoofing, front-running, and non-competitive trading as prohibited. Rulebook Chapter 5 covers fraudulent, non-competitive, unfair, or abusive practices. Rule numbering shifts between versions; a recent filing cites Rule 5.17 for self-matching, wash trading, and pre-arranged trading. — [Kalshi FAQ](https://kalshi.com/faq); [Kalshi rule filing (CFTC)](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09152626141.pdf)
- In Sept 2026 Kalshi denied wash-trading allegations about its ETH perpetuals. It said it mechanically blocks self-trades and surveils for pre-arranged trades. — [iGaming Business roundup (2026-09-25)](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/prediction-market-roundup-92526/)
- **Market maker program:** selective, with fee and position-limit perks. Trades between designated market makers with advanced API access may be adjusted if caused by a trading-software error. Kalshi also revised its "trader error" rules as it courts institutions. — [Kalshi Help Center](https://help.kalshi.com/markets/market-maker-program); [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/kalshi-tweaks-trader-error-rules-180921802.html)

**Polymarket rules**
- Polymarket published enhanced market-integrity rules on 2026-03-23 covering both the international DeFi platform and the US DCM.

The Enforcement Director called the view that insider-trading law doesn't apply to prediction markets "flatly wrong." — [Sullivan & Cromwell (Apr 2026)](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/April/CFTC-Updates-Enforcement-Priorities-Cooperation-Policy-Prediction-Markets-Insider-Trading); [Greenberg Traurig (Apr 2026)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/4/prediction-markets-a-cftc-enforcement-update)

- On 2026-04-22 Kalshi suspended and fined three congressional candidates (MN, TX, VA) for trading on their own races. — [CNBC (2026-04-22)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/22/kalshi-insider-trading-congress.html)

### Inferences
- **Bot design implications** (my synthesis, not legal advice):
  - Never let the bot trade against itself across accounts. Kalshi blocks self-matches, but cross-account matching can look pre-arranged.
  - Avoid rapid place-and-cancel layering that a surveillance system could read as spoofing.
  - Respect rate limits.
  - Don't resell or share Kalshi API data.
  - Run one account per person; multi-accounting is a common ToS breach.
  - Do not feed the bot non-public information. This includes employer data, leaked results, or advance knowledge from your job, because misappropriation theory reaches anyone who breaches a duty of confidence.
  - Avoid "mention" and niche markets where you or your contacts could influence or know the outcome.
- An LLM agent that scrapes or ingests data could inadvertently use MNPI (for example, from a paywalled or embargoed feed). Logging data provenance is a prudent defense.
- Exchange disciplinary actions (bans plus multiples of trade size) can be imposed without the CFTC. Account loss is the most likely sanction for a careless bot.

## 5. Taxes for a US individual (tax year 2026)

### Takeaway
As of late 2026 the IRS has issued no specific guidance on prediction-market contracts. Practitioners split among four treatments:
- ordinary "other income" (most conservative)
- gambling (§165(d))
- capital gains (Form 8949)
- §1256 60/40 (widely seen as aggressive)

Platforms generally do not issue a 1099 reporting event-contract profits, but all net income is reportable regardless. Two 2026-specific issues stand out. First, the 2025 tax law caps gambling-loss deductions at 90% beginning in 2026 (the FAIR BET Act repeal is pending, not law). Second, Polymarket international adds a crypto-property layer, where every USDC movement can be a taxable disposal. A high-volume bot makes recordkeeping and a trader-tax CPA essential.

### Cited Findings
- **No IRS guidance; candidate treatments.** Sources: [Beancount (2026-09-07)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide); [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [PredictionRanks](https://predictionranks.com/prediction-markets/prediction-market-tax-guide-2026); [Advora](https://advorahq.com/kalshi-polymarket-taxes/)
  - **Ordinary income:** net profit on Schedule 1, Line 8z, taxed at 10–37% federal. Called the most defensible default.
  - **Gambling (§165(d)):** winnings are reported gross. Losses are deductible only if itemizing (Schedule A), only up to winnings, and with no carryforward.
  - **Capital gains:** Form 8949, no itemizing needed.
  - **§1256:** rests on the "CFTC-regulated exchange" argument. Many practitioners say the contract itself must be a regulated futures contract, so a written opinion from a trader-tax CPA is recommended before taking this position.
  - A Tax Notes Federal analysis (co-authored with a former head of the IRS Office of Digital Assets) frames this as an open capital-or-ordinary question.
- **90% loss cap:** the 2025 tax law cut the wagering-loss deduction to 90% of losses starting in 2026 (deduction = lesser of 90% of losses or winnings), scored at over $1B through 2034. The FAIR BET Act would restore 100%. A repeal passed a House committee but was not law as of late Sept 2026. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide); [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)
- **Forms issued (sources conflict; verify against your own account):**
  - **Kalshi:** provides a downloadable P&L statement, not a 1099 showing trading profit. It may issue a 1099-MISC for referral or reward credits of $600 or more, a 1099-INT for interest of $10 or more, and a 1099-B or 1099-DA only for limited broker or digital-asset transactions. One guide (Monaco CPA) says Kalshi issues a 1099-MISC for annual net profits, which contradicts the others. — [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide); [Monaco CPA](https://www.monacocpa.cpa/prediction-market-tax)
  - **Polymarket international:** no 1099-B, 1099-DA, or W-2G; you must self-report. **Polymarket US:** 1099 status "not confirmed" per one table. — [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [MarketMath](https://marketmath.io/blog/how-prediction-markets-taxed)
  - **Robinhood:** one guide says it issues no 1099 for event contracts. This is a single secondary source. — [MarketMath](https://marketmath.io/blog/how-prediction-markets-taxed)
  - A missing 1099 does not remove the reporting obligation. — [CountOnSheep](https://countonsheep.com/blog/prediction-market-taxes); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide)
- **Crypto/USDC on Polymarket:** the IRS treats crypto as property, so funding, token conversions, and stablecoin settlement can each create separate gain or loss on top of the contract result. You need per-unit cost basis; a year-end CSV is insufficient. One commentator argues every purchase, sale, and redemption is a digital-asset disposal. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide); [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)
- **Trader status and §475:** trader status is a strict facts-and-circumstances test, and courts have denied it even to taxpayers with hundreds of trades a year. The §475 mark-to-market election must generally be made by the prior year's return due date and is revocable only with IRS consent. Whether event contracts qualify is unresolved. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide)
- **Recordkeeping recommendations:**
  - Export trade, settlement, and statement files monthly and keep the raw files.
  - Log each market's dates, contract, quantity, prices, fees, and net result.
  - Track gross winnings and gross losses separately (needed if gambling treatment is used).
  - Keep wallet and bank records and notes on promotions.
  - Reconcile quarterly.
  - Pay estimated tax (Form 1040-ES), since there is no withholding.
  - Source: [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide)

### Inferences
- **Wash-sale rule** (my inference; no source addressed it directly): §1091 applies to losses on "stock or securities." Event contracts on a DCM are CFTC-regulated derivatives, not securities, so the wash-sale rule likely does not apply. Under a gambling characterization it is irrelevant anyway. Confirm with a CPA.
- **§1256 tension** (my inference): the CFTC's Oct 2026 NPRM seeks to classify event contracts as "swaps." Post-Dodd-Frank, §1256(b)(2)(B) excludes swaps and "similar agreements" from §1256 contract treatment. The regulatory push toward "swap" status therefore arguably weakens the 60/40 argument further. This needs professional confirmation.
- **Bot-specific consequences:**
  - Thousands of small trades make per-trade gross-win and gross-loss tracking essential if gambling treatment is ever used. Gambling treatment now penalizes a break-even bot: for example, $50k winnings and $50k losses leaves $5k taxable at the 90% cap.
  - Ordinary or capital treatment nets more naturally.
  - Whatever position is taken should be applied consistently across years.
- **Bot P&L ledger:** the bot should write its own immutable ledger (timestamps, market IDs, fills, fees, and for Polymarket, on-chain tx hashes and USDC cost basis) independent of platform exports. Platforms can lose history or change export formats.

### Gaps
- I found no IRS publication, notice, or ruling on prediction markets, and no published Tax Court case.
- The status of the FAIR BET Act after Sept 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Polymarket US's and Robinhood's 2026 information-reporting practices are unconfirmed (sources conflict or are silent).
- State tax treatment (for example, states that don't allow gambling-loss deductions) was not researched.

## 6. Non-market risks: resolution/oracle, custody/counterparty, wallet security, account freezes, KYC

### Takeaway
Resolution risk is real on both platforms.
- **Polymarket international:** relies on UMA's token-holder-voted optimistic oracle. That oracle has produced several high-profile outcomes contrary to reported facts. Voting power is concentrated, voters have had conflicts of interest, and disputes are rising (1,150+ in Jan–May 2026). Disputed settlements have been litigated.
- **Kalshi:** resolves by its own rulebook. It has invoked fine-print carve-outs (the Feb 2026 Khamenei "death carveout," about $54M, now a class action), reversed payout decisions after backlash, paid wrong winners, and been forced by a state court to unwind trades.

Custody risk differs sharply:
- Kalshi and Polymarket US hold fiat in segregated accounts at affiliated, CFTC-registered clearinghouses and FCMs. Commentators and competitors have raised conflict and customer-priority concerns.
- Polymarket international is self-custody USDC on Polygon. A bot holding a hot private key is a direct theft vector, and there is no FCM or bankruptcy protection.

### Inferences
- **Resolution risk is a first-class model input**, not noise. A bot should:
  - parse and store each market's full rules and any clarifications at entry
  - avoid or size down markets with ambiguous wording, "death/act-of-God" carve-outs, or subjective criteria
  - expect payout delays of days on disputed Polymarket markets
  - remember that platforms can settle at "last traded price" or void contracts
- **Counterparty risk** for a small account on Kalshi or Polymarket US is mainly affiliate-structure and operational risk, not outright default. Keeping only working capital on platform is prudent.
- **Self-custody (non-US users or Polymarket international):**
  - A bot holding a private key on a server is a single point of failure.
  - Mitigations include a dedicated low-balance hot wallet, limited token approvals, periodic sweeps to cold storage, secrets management rather than plaintext env files, and no key material in LLM prompts or logs.
  - I did not find a source on prediction-market-specific bot key thefts, so this is general crypto-security practice.

## 7. Briefly: how the picture differs for non-US residents

### Takeaway
Non-US residents can generally use Polymarket international (self-custody, crypto-settled, with no US-style KYC for basic use). However, many major jurisdictions are now "close-only" or blocked: the UK, France, Germany, Italy, Belgium, Poland, Australia, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, and several Canadian provinces, per a Nov 2026 compilation. Several European regulators have actively blocked the site (France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Portugal). Local gambling, financial, and tax rules then govern.

### Cited Findings
All from [AirdropAlert (2026-11-08)](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/polymarket-restricted-countries/), which attributes its list to Polymarket's geoblock docs. Note the source's own count discrepancy (it claims 36 close-only countries but lists 33).
- **Fully blocked:** Iran, Syria, Cuba, North Korea, and the Crimea, Donetsk, and Luhansk regions.
- **Close-only (website and API):** Belarus, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Russia, Slovakia, UK, Argentina, Brazil, Nicaragua, Venezuela, the US, Alberta, British Columbia, Ontario, Quebec, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, and several Middle East and African states.
- **Close-only on website only:** Ireland, Japan, Netherlands, South Korea; Malta (sports only).
- **Regulator actions:**
  - France: financial transactions banned Nov 2024; ISP block ordered 2026-07-16.
  - Italy: ADM blacklisted the site Oct 2025 and blocked it again 2026-07-27.
  - Belgium: blacklisted.
  - Switzerland: Gespa blocklisted the site 2024-11-26 and added copycat domains Aug 2026.
  - Portugal: SRIJ ordered Polymarket out within 48 hours in Jan 2026.

### Inferences
- A non-US resident in an unrestricted country faces mostly crypto-custody, oracle, and local-tax risk rather than access risk. Residents of the UK and much of the EU are effectively excluded from opening new Polymarket positions, and VPN workarounds carry the same freeze risk as for US users.

### Gaps
- Kalshi's international availability as of Nov 2026 (it reportedly expanded beyond the US) was not researched.
- UK FCA/Gambling Commission treatment of prediction markets and EU MiCA implications were not researched.
- The country list rests on a single compiled secondary source dated 2026-11-08. Polymarket's live geoblock endpoint should be checked directly.
